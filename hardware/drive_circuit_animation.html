SMA Drive Circuit

The corrected circuit that heats the NiTi wire, with a coil that smooths the current, a shunt on the low side, a fuse and a bulk capacitor. It replays the simulated up-stroke to maximum trailing-edge deflection (target TE −42.2 mm), on the wire model you choose, so you can see what every part does at each moment.

Scope at real speed

Four cycles of 50 µs each. The top trace is the voltage at the MOSFET drain. The bottom trace is the current in the wire and coil. The chip reads the shunt in the middle of the on-time (dot), where the ripple current equals its average.

Ratings are example values I chose, not datasheet numbers. “Bare” means no heatsink: 40 K/W and a 100 K rise gives about 2.5 W. A part above 100 % needs a heatsink, a bigger part or a different topology.

Two loops on the chip

Order of events only, durations are not to scale. The slow loop is sma.c, which is written and tested against the Python model. The fast loop needs the board layer, which is not written yet.

What changed

  1. This version. A banner says the numbers are model numbers. The wire model is selectable (nominal, ANSYS-fitted, convection and window errors). The wing shows the corrugated trailing edge at true scale. A safety-monitor panel replays injected faults. A margin panel checks every part against an example rating. A strip shows the slow and fast loops.
  2. A coil and a freewheel path. The wire has almost no inductance, so a bare MOSFET gives a square current with peaks near 80 A. Heating follows the RMS value, which is higher than the average the controller assumes. The coil keeps the current smooth.
  3. The shunt moved to the source side. It sits near ground, so a simple amplifier can read it. The chip reads it in the middle of the on-time.
  4. A fuse and a bulk capacitor. The fuse stops a runaway current if the MOSFET fails on. The capacitor supplies the switching pulses close to the MOSFET.
  5. The relay sits between supply and wire. The chip turns the MOSFET off first and opens the relay second, so the contacts never break 35 A.
  6. Thermocouples are insulated from the wire. Bonded thermally but not electrically, otherwise the wire's own voltage drop corrupts the reading.
  7. The freewheel diode gets hot. At 35 A it loses about 10 W, four times what a bare part can shed. The page now uses a second MOSFET switched opposite to the first (synchronous rectification), about 1.4 W. The toggle in the margins panel shows both.
